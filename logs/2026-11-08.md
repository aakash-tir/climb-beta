## Add grey-world white balance with wall reference (#3)

**What we did:** Added `climb_beta.colour.white_balance`: `grey_world_gains(img, ref_mask)`, `apply_gains`, `grey_world`. Gains come from the reference pixels (the wall, when a mask is given), ignore clipped highlights, fall back to the whole image when the reference is tiny, and are clipped to [0.5, 2].

**The reason:** Gym LEDs tint everything warm, and that shifts every hold's hue and saturation before the colour rules see it. A plain whole-image grey-world is biased by the holds themselves, since they're deliberately saturated and often mostly warm colours.

**How it was solved:** The test that matters crowds a wall with red/orange/yellow holds under a warm tint. Wall-referenced gains bring the wall to within 4 levels of neutral and beat whole-image gains on it. Highlights at 250+ are excluded because a clipped channel says nothing about the light. No diagram change (the diagram lands with #25).

## Add chalk-aware per-pixel colour family voting (#4)

**What we did:** Added `climb_beta.colour.rules`: `hold_pixels` (HSV + Lab inside the mask, chalk flag), `pixel_labels` (black / white / grey / hue family per pixel), and `classify_hold` / `classify_pixels`, which vote over non-chalk pixels and return a `ColourResult` with name, confidence, votes, chalk fraction and a reason. Families resolve to colours through a `FAMILY_RESOLVERS` hook that #5 (pink/red) plugs into. Synthetic yellow was moved to a truer yellow (hue ≈ 52°).

**The reason:** This is the core of the rule-based colour step. Chalk is the main failure mode, because averaging chalked pixels washes any colour out, so chalk pixels get no vote at all.

**How it was solved:** Labels are assigned in priority order: hue family, then achromatic (S < 60) as white/grey by V, then black (V < 70) overrides everything, since hue is meaningless in shadow. Tests: seven plain colours, blue chalked 30/60/80% centre-first, scattered chalk on green, and a sweep of the whole hue circle with no gaps. A fully chalked hold returns `unknown` until the white edge-ring rule (#6) lands. No diagram change.

## Split pink vs red by saturation and lightness (#5)

**What we did:** Added `split_pink_red`, the redpink family resolver: take the hold's red-pink pixels, keep the top quantile by saturation (0.75), and call it red if their median S ≥ 190 and median Lab L* ≤ 150, otherwise pink. `tests/synth.chalk` gained `blend`, which mixes chalk with the pigment at a random opacity per pixel the way thin chalk does.

**The reason:** A chalked red hold's voting pixels look pink on average: the mean S of the non-chalk pixels falls to 190 / 154 / 124 at 30 / 60 / 80% chalk, so a mean-based rule calls all three pink. That's measured, not assumed. Hue can't separate the two either, since both sit near 0°/360°, especially under warm light.

**How it was solved:** Chalk and glare only lower saturation and raise lightness, so the most saturated pixels are the closest to bare pigment. Tests: clean red / hot pink / light pink; red chalked 30–80% (centre-first and scattered, with blended chalk) is never pink; chalked pink stays pink; red and pink built with the *same hue* split correctly at hues 0, 175 and 178; and red and pink stay apart under warm light after wall-referenced white balance. Known limit: a deep saturated pink would read as red. Filed as 36 for re-tuning on real crops. No diagram change.

## Add white-hold rule using the mask's edge ring (#6)

**What we did:** Added the white rule to `classify_hold`. When at least 55% of a mask is chalk-like, look at its 4 px inner edge ring. If the ring is mostly bright achromatic (≥ 60%) **and** almost free of chromatic pixels (≤ 15%), the hold is white. Otherwise it's a chalked coloured hold, and the vote is taken on the ring alone. New config key: `white_ring.ring_colour_frac_max`.

**The reason:** The chalk filter alone deletes a white hold, since every pixel is "chalk". The edge ring is the hold's sides, which hands rarely touch, so it separates a white hold from a coloured hold covered in chalk.

**How it was solved:** The first version used only the white fraction, and it failed the test it should fail: red and blue holds 92% chalked centre-first came out "white", because only about a 1 px band of pigment survives inside the 4 px ring. The fix asks the right question: a real white hold has *no* pigment anywhere, so more than 15% chromatic ring pixels means coloured. Tests: white holds at radius 12/30/50, chalked white, red/blue at 85% and 92% chalk (voted on ring), grey, and a bright light-grey hold whose body looks like chalk but whose ring is dimmer, so it stays grey. No diagram change.

## Add Basecamp photo layout and split-by-wall (#13)

**What we did:** Added `climb_beta.data.split` (`assign_walls`, `validate_splits`, load/save `splits.yaml`, `scan_walls`, `build_dataset`), the `data/split_by_wall.py assign|build` CLI, and the committed Basecamp layout: `data/basecamp/{raw,labels}/.gitkeep`, `walls.yaml` and a README. `.gitignore` now ignores the *contents* of `raw/` and `labels/`. ## Add timm crop classifier and training script (#21)

**What we did:** Added `climb_beta.colour.classifier` (numpy `preprocess`, validated `CheckpointMeta`, `build_model` for timm `mobilenetv3_large_100` / `resnet18`, and `CropClassifier` for inference) and `colour/train_classifier.py`. **The reason:** The second half of the colour step from the brief: a small learned classifier on labelled crops, for the cases the rules can't separate on real photos.

**How it was solved:** - **Colour is the label, so augmentation never touches hue or saturation**: flips, 90° rotations, and one brightness gain applied to all channels. A test checks that hue is unchanged across 20 augmentations.

- `ml` smoke test: 3 epochs of ResNet18 on synthetic crops on the GPU, then reload and predict. Passed.

## Add route grouping and tape-start detection (#23)

**What we did:** Added `climb_beta.route.group` (`group_routes`, `RouteResult.named_route`, `load_route_config`) and `configs/route.yaml`. Holds of the chosen colour are linked when their mask gap is ≤ `link_factor` × the median hold size, and connected components become routes. A hold whose dilated mask touches a tape strip within `tape_reach_factor` × hold size is a start. Clusters smaller than `min_route_holds` are reported as strays.

**The reason:** The route step from the brief: group the holds of a chosen colour, and find tape near holds to mark the start. The exit criterion "named route correct within 1 hold" also needs a rule for which route a colour *names* when a wall has two of that colour.

**How it was solved:** Distances are in units of the photo's own median hold size, so the grouping doesn't depend on resolution or shooting distance (tested by doubling hold size and spacing). `named_route` picks the single taped route if there is one, otherwise the largest, because the start tape is how a climber identifies "the red one" on a busy wall. Mask gaps are computed only for pairs whose boxes are within reach, on the crop of their union box, so it stays cheap on 12 MP photos. A far-away hold of the same colour, typically a colour mistake, becomes a stray instead of an extra route hold, which protects the ±1-hold criterion. Tests: two red routes split, blue ignored, tape marks only the adjacent red hold (not a nearby blue one), the taped smaller route beats the larger untaped one, a far stray is excluded. No diagram change yet; the diagram lands in #25 once the pipeline (#24) joins the pieces.

## Add end-to-end pipeline and route CLI (#24)

**What we did:** Added `climb_beta.pipeline.run`: photo → detector → wall-referenced white balance → colour per hold (rules, or `method="classifier"`) → routes for the chosen colours, returning a `PipelineResult` with JSON output. **The reason:** The app and the exit-criteria eval need one call from photo to routes, built from the same pieces each uses, so what's demoed is what's measured.

**How it was solved:** The pipeline only wires existing tested pieces. `wall_scene()` is the first test of all three steps on one image: chalked red holds, warm light and start tape at once. It expects the named red route to be exactly the 5 red holds with the taped one as start, and blue's route to be its 4 holds with no start.
